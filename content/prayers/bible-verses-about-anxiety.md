Anxiety has a way of narrowing the world down to whatever is wrong right now. Scripture doesn't pretend that worry isn't real, but it keeps pointing us back to a God who is near enough to hand it to.

"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus" (Philippians 4:6-7). Paul doesn't say don't feel anxious — he says take it to God before it takes root, and let thanksgiving keep company with the request.

Peter puts it even more simply: "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you" (1 Peter 5:7). Casting is an active word — it's a decision to set something down rather than keep carrying it, made easier by remembering that He actually cares what happens to you.

The Psalms give us honest language for the in-between moments too: "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul" (Psalm 94:19). Even when the mind is loud, there is room for comfort to arrive.

And Jesus speaks directly to the worry that circles around tomorrow: "Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof" (Matthew 6:34). Today has enough to hold without borrowing tomorrow's weight in advance.

Lord, You know how quickly my thoughts spiral when I try to manage everything on my own. Teach me to bring You each worry as it comes instead of stockpiling them in silence. Steady my heart with a peace I cannot manufacture myself, and remind me, again and again, that You are already carrying what I hand to You. Amen.
