Hard times rarely announce themselves politely. They arrive as a diagnosis, a loss, a bill you can't cover, a silence where an answer should be — and suddenly the ground you stood on doesn't feel steady anymore. Scripture was written by people who knew exactly what that felt like, and it doesn't ask us to pretend otherwise.

"The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit" (Psalm 34:18). God is not distant from your hard season — He is described as near precisely when the heart is breaking, not after it has healed.

Paul, writing from a Roman prison, could still say: "We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed" (2 Corinthians 4:8-9). Notice he doesn't deny the trouble — he names it plainly. What he refuses is the conclusion that trouble gets the last word.

And there's this steady promise from Isaiah: "Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness" (Isaiah 41:10). Four small promises stacked together — strengthen, help, uphold — for exactly the days when you have none of that left in yourself.

Even the disciples heard Jesus say it plainly: "In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33). He never promised a life free of hard times. He promised He'd already met them and come through.

If you're in one of those seasons now, you don't have to explain it away or rush toward a lesson in it. It's enough to be honest about how heavy it is, and to let these words remind you Who is holding the other end.

Lord, this season is heavier than I know how to carry well. I don't need You to explain it to me right now — I just need to know You're near while I'm in it. Strengthen what's weak in me, steady what's shaking, and uphold me when I run out of my own resolve. Thank You that hard times are not the end of the story You're writing. Amen.
